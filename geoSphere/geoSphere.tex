\section{Planar graphs}
Drawing the planar graph of a polyhedron is a useful way to study its structure. In particular, it makes it easy to count the number of faces, vertices, and edges. 

To obtain the planar graph of a polyhedron, imagine placing it on a table, stretching the top, and pushing it down onto the tabletop so that none of the edges cross.

\section{From the icosahedron to the football}
A geodesic polyhedron is a polyhedron whose vertices lie on a sphere and whose faces are obtained by subdividing the faces of an icosahedron into smaller polygons while remaining close to the spherical shape.

A particularly famous example is the football (also called the truncated icosahedron), whose faces consist of pentagons and hexagons.

Before studying the football, begin with the dodecahedron.

\section{Building a GeoSphere}
Start with an icosahedron. Subdivide each triangular face into smaller triangles by joining the midpoints of its edges, so that every face is split into four triangles. Next, truncate the original vertices of the icosahedron. The resulting polyhedron has pentagonal faces where the original vertices were and hexagonal faces everywhere else. This is the familiar football pattern and is the first member of a family of Goldberg polyhedra.
